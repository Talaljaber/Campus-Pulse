## Decision grammar and apply rules
- `keep`, `fix:<Category>/<priority>` (exact category spelling, case-insensitive), `drop`; blank = undecided.
- `needs_human_check=False` is set only for: rows with a decision of keep or fix; the non-queue EN/AR siblings of a
  scenario whose `scope=scenario` row was kept or fixed; and, with `--accept-agreed`, checker-agreed rows (below).
- A fix on any row of a new scenario sets the label of every form of that scenario (the validator needs one label per
  scenario), including undecided Arabizi forms, which stay `needs_human_check=True`. Two different fixes in one
  scenario abort the run.
- `drop` on a `scope=scenario` row drops every form of the scenario (README rule: forms stay only if not dropped, and a
  scenario's verdict is its anchor's); `drop` on any other row drops only that row.
- `fix:` on paraphrases, translations and Arabizi files is refused (labels come from the source scenario): `drop` an
  unfaithful row; `keep` plus `edit_text` fixes wording.
- Bad cells, conflicts and refusals abort before any file is written. It never touches `data/raw` or `data/clean`.

## Fast path and its risk
`apply --accept-agreed` additionally accepts every undecided row whose checker agreed (drafter label = checker label,
natural), except Arabizi (`--include-arabizi` adds them) and except rows of a scenario whose labels were fixed.
Guard (bypass with `--skip-guard`): every T1-T3 row decided, and at most 10 % of the user's decisions on checker-agreed
queue rows (T2 anchors, T3 sample) were fix or drop. Risk: the unread agreed rows (about 209 new-scenario forms plus
paraphrases) are trusted because a different model agreed and the user's sample was clean; two LLMs can share a wrong
reading of a borderline priority (the medium/high "today" cases), and sibling forms are not read individually.
